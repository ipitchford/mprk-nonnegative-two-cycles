# Recorded replay

On 20 October 2026, during publication preparation, a copy of this package
(Python 3.14.7, macOS 27.0.1, arm64) ran

    python3 -S -B -O verify_all.py

All seven certificates C1–C7 regenerated byte for byte (C4 706.6 s, C6 66.0 s,
C7 17.3 s, the others about 1 s each). `negative_controls.py` behaved as
expected in normal and optimised mode: the trapping test holds at the
counterexample and fails for the symmetric system and for alpha = 1/2, 2/3 and
1; a tampered certificate and an edited library are detected; the verifiers
stop with a CertificateError on p = 1/2 and on a31 = 113/222; and no
acceptance test uses `assert`.

Earlier on the same day a separate clean replay of the approved research
package (Python 3.13, macOS) also regenerated C1–C7 byte for byte. The
publication gate repeats the replay on the frozen release archive; its private
receipt binds the archive hash. CI repeats the fast replay on Python 3.10 and
3.13 and the full replay on Python 3.13; its results are visible in the
repository's Actions tab.

These replays show that the archived certificates are exactly what the
archived code produces. They are not proof-assistant verification, external
peer review or independent reproduction.
